By Oscar Wilde

Themes

Life & Death, Beauty & Appearance, Morality & Corruption

Synopsis

The Picture of Dorian Gray by Oscar Wilde follows Dorian Gray, a handsome young man who wishes that a portrait of him would grow old instead of him. His wish mysteriously comes true, allowing Dorian to remain young while the portrait reflects the effects of his selfish and immoral actions.

Influenced by Lord Henry Wotton, Dorian becomes obsessed with pleasure and beauty. As he makes increasingly harmful choices, his portrait becomes more disturbing, while his appearance remains unchanged. Eventually, Dorian must face the consequences of the life he has chosen.

The story explores themes of beauty, youth, morality, temptation, vanity, and the consequences of one's actions. Dorian's relationship with the portrait becomes increasingly disturbing as he realizes that it records every change in his character. Although he tries to hide his darker side from society, the portrait serves as a constant reminder of the person he has become.

As Dorian's life continues, his desire to remain young and free from consequences leads him further into corruption. His attempts to escape his past ultimately bring him face-to-face with the portrait and the consequences of his choices.
